How to Import Products from India: A Complete Step-by-Step Guide

Why Import from India?

India is the world's manufacturing hub for a wide range of goods. With over 63 million MSMEs and a government actively supporting export growth through schemes like RoDTEP and PLI, Indian products offer unmatched quality-to-price ratios. From agri-commodities to industrial goods, buying from India gives you a competitive edge.

Step 1: Identify Your Product & HS Code

Every traded product has a Harmonized System (HS) code — a 6 to 10-digit number used globally for customs classification. Knowing the HS code for your product helps you check import duties in your country, find Indian suppliers registered for that category, and ensure compliance with regulations.

Step 2: Find & Verify Indian Suppliers

Use verified platforms like Bharat TradeLink, IndiaMart, or TradeIndia to find suppliers. Always verify with IEC number check (dgft.gov.in), GSTIN verification (gst.gov.in), factory audit or third-party inspection, and sample orders before bulk purchase.

Step 3: Request Quotation & Negotiate Terms

Request a pro-forma invoice with pricing on FOB/CIF basis, payment terms (T/T 30% advance, balance against BL copy is common), lead time and production capacity, and certifications available (ISO, CE, FSSAI, etc.).

Step 4: Handle Documentation & Compliance

Key export documents from India include: Commercial Invoice & Packing List, Bill of Lading / Airway Bill, Certificate of Origin (from FIEO or local chamber), Phytosanitary Certificate (for agri goods), and Fumigation Certificate (for wooden packing). At Bharat TradeLink, we handle all documentation on your behalf.

Step 5: Arrange Shipping & Insurance

For sea freight (most common for bulk goods), transit times range from 7 days (UAE) to 30 days (USA East Coast). Always insure your cargo — marine insurance costs 0.1–0.3% of CIF value and protects against loss, damage, or piracy.

💡 Pro Tip: Always conduct a pre-shipment inspection (PSI) for orders above $10,000. Third-party agencies like SGS, Bureau Veritas, or Intertek provide affordable inspections that dramatically reduce the risk of quality disputes.
